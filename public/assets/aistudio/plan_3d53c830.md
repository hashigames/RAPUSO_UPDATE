# RAPUSO — implementation and design plan

RAPUSO is an English–Japanese browser animation studio by **LIGHTHOUSE 橋**. It creates asset-based anime-style animation videos, pose images and drawing-reference sequences. Uploaded code is reference material, not a requirement to preserve its implementation. The existing templates depend on missing supporting modules and contain hardcoded administrative behavior; the new implementation will retain useful workflow ideas without copying those shortcuts.

## Product approach

The animation engine owns character appearance, environment imagery, props, materials, camera and saved motion. AI interprets instructions into supported motion/pose operations and can optionally refine a rendered still into concept art. It does not redraw every animation frame. Saved scene and animation data provide consistency; they do not guarantee correct physics or arbitrary cinematic choreography.

The supplied videos are style and action references, not imported rigged scenes. Their cel-shaded characters, industrial settings, platforming and tracking cameras inform the studio. Visible rope/grip/contact issues illustrate why asset attachment and explicit constraints matter. An MP4 cannot supply the original meshes, skeleton, environment geometry or animation tracks.

## Required workflows

### Animation studio

A real interactive Three.js viewport renders a bundled, clearly labeled demonstration rig or uploaded characters. Users can orbit/frame the camera, inspect the skeleton and edit actual bone rotations. Rig inspection reports skinned meshes, bones, embedded clips and missing skinning. Editing an elbow must affect the actual bound mesh, not only a marker.

Support self-contained GLB, FBX and bundled glTF character imports; GLB is recommended for embedded assets. OBJ and unrigged meshes remain static scene/prop assets. Resolve external resources from selected companion files and report missing dependencies. No automatic high-quality rigging of arbitrary meshes is promised.

Users can add and arrange environment images, static meshes and props. An image background is not advertised as a complete 3D environment. Props can attach to a selected character bone with editable offset, rotation and scale. Material selection allows original, toon, clay and line/wire reference treatments, with color and texture changes where supported. Original materials/assets remain recoverable.

The timeline combines supported motion presets, imported clips and saved pose keyframes. Users can change clip length/order, playback speed, camera views and loop behavior. AI direction translates an English or Japanese prompt into a validated, editable sequence drawn from the available actions and actual rig capabilities. Unsupported instructions produce an explanation and required inputs, not invented motion. Imported embedded clips are replayed as animation data rather than regenerated.

The render workspace produces real video and frame files from the scene. Provide sequences longer than the 30-second example, including one-minute sequences, subject to browser resources. Show duration, frame rate, format support, progress and cancellation. Use native browser video recording with a supported WebM output and MP4 where the available encoder permits it; never label WebM bytes as MP4 or promise universal codec support. Rendering speed is measured, not guaranteed. Frame-sequence exports use PNG images in ZIP with a scene/settings manifest.

### Pose and drawing-reference studio

Users can choose a pose preset, manipulate available bones, save/reload poses and export rendered PNGs. Drawing/reference modes offer toon, clay and line/wire views. Contact-sheet or sequential-frame exports come from the same saved assets and motion. Optional AI concept-art refinement uses the rendered still and the user's instructions, with an explicit warning that generated art may reinterpret details and is not an identity-exact animation export.

### Recorded-reference workspace

Users can import and play recorded MP4/WebM references, seek to timestamps and capture stills. Provide real experimental single-person body tracking with MediaPipe Pose Landmarker and retarget tracked body directions to mapped humanoid bones. Allow sampling/progress/cancellation and manual joint mapping. Explain that occlusions, camera motion, stylized/non-human anatomy and missing full-body visibility can prevent useful transfer. Facial/finger/cloth motion, exact contact and production-quality reconstruction are not claimed. Track confidence and report insufficient results rather than substituting a fake animation.

Existing AI-video assistance is limited to supported reference analysis, trim/range selection, visual adjustments and reference-frame capture. Anatomical or foot-contact repair is not exposed as a guaranteed operation. A future generative-video provider integration must be configured and tested before the app can offer that capability; agent-only video tools are not assumed to be callable by this application.

### Assets, projects and guidance

Provide an asset library for characters, environments, props, textures/material references and recorded videos. Keep local working assets in browser storage, with project-file export/import for portability. Add authenticated cloud project records and durable asset upload through the initialized managed backend. Cloud records are isolated by authenticated user; local use must not require pretending to be logged in. Cloud asset removal is described as removal from the library, not guaranteed physical deletion.

Save scene settings, asset identifiers, motion/keyframe data, reference selections and language. Provide undoable pose reset and recoverable originals. Validate file types, file/resource sizes and finite transforms. Keep service keys server-side. Use the application's real existing OAuth/session foundation without copying Firebase credentials or hardcoded admin access from the templates.

Throughout the app show actionable missing-input messages, such as “This mesh has no skin weights; upload a skinned character to pose its elbow,” “Supply a full-body reference for body tracking,” or “This action needs an animation clip.” No fake generation completion or fictitious AI consistency guarantee.

## Implementation and project structure

Use the initialized React/TypeScript frontend and Express/tRPC backend. Three.js supplies the renderer/loaders/animation mixer. MediaPipe supplies optional browser body-pose inference. ZIP tooling packages frame and project exports. Server-side managed LLM and image services provide the supported AI features; use current model discovery and validated structured results.

- `server/`: protected project/asset procedures and AI direction/art endpoints.
- `drizzle/`: additive project/asset schema and migrations.
- `client/public/`: small brand assets and route declarations; large uploaded/reference media use durable storage.

The configured development listener is port 3000. The provided Docker-based backend serves the frontend and `/api/health`. Complete language diagnostics, relevant unit/type/build checks and one read-only implementation review before saving a managed checkpoint and delivering the live preview. Publishing to a broad audience is separate from creating the preview and requires authorization unless the user enables automatic publication.

## Design

**Design movement:** a restrained dark production workstation, based on the supplied standalone-studio header, with cinematic contact-sheet and editing-suite details.

**Core principles:** the canvas leads; technical controls stay legible; appearance and motion are visibly separate; limitations are part of the workflow rather than hidden in marketing.

**Color philosophy:** charcoal and ink-black panels support accurate visual review. Muted rose highlights creative direction and the active workspace. Soft white carries primary text, with restrained green for verified rig/readiness information.

**Layout paradigm:** persistent narrow navigation rail, a broad canvas/editor center, a contextual right inspector and a bottom timeline. Project/asset browsing uses an editorial list with visual previews rather than a generic analytics dashboard. Smaller screens stack inspectors and retain access to core controls.

**Signature elements:** italic RAPUSO wordmark, fine borders and segmented timeline tracks, compact bilingual labels and readable timecode.

**Interaction philosophy:** direct manipulation, labeled control states, reversible adjustments and clear export status. Actions without the required asset or capability explain the missing dependency.

**Animation:** restrained hover/selection transitions; canvas motion follows saved animation time. No decorative motion that confuses rendering progress or masks a non-working control.

**Typography:** Barlow Condensed for the expressive wordmark and major studio headings, Inter for controls, JetBrains Mono for timecode/technical metadata, and Noto Sans JP for Japanese coverage.

**Brand essence:** an asset-first animation and reference studio for artists who want repeatable control. Precise, creative, candid.

**Brand voice:** plain studio instructions: “Direct the motion. Keep the character.” and “Need a skinned mesh to bend this joint.” Japanese UI conveys the same meaning, not just translated navigation.

**Wordmark and logo:** use RAPUSO as explicitly requested, despite the RASUPO spelling in the example. Display **RAPUSO / ラプソ** and preserve **LIGHTHOUSE 橋** company attribution and the supplied logo.

**Signature brand color:** muted rose `#E6A1B0` against graphite, matching the supplied reference's creative-editor accent.

## Boundaries to approve

This is a functional asset-based animation/reference tool, not a promise to reproduce the cinematic quality and complex physics of the examples automatically from arbitrary prompts. High-quality output depends on supplied meshes, skinning, animation data and suitable environments. Experimental recorded-video body transfer and optional AI concept-art refinement are explicitly labeled. Native iOS/Android apps, general-purpose auto-rigging, automatic detailed physical interaction and exact generative-video repairs are not included without additional capability work.

Reference for body-tracking API: https://ai.google.dev/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker/web_js
